# Cover letter draft

To the Editor, Evolutionary Bioinformatics

Dear Editor,

Please consider “Contact-Network Remodeling During Bacterial Sheath Contraction Is
Associated with Site-Specific Evolutionary Rates in an AFP System” as an Original Research
Article. The study asks whether intermolecular contact exchange between experimentally
observed sheath conformations contains site-specific evolutionary-rate information beyond
monomer accessibility, packing and flexibility averaged across those states.

The structural comparison covers five proteins from four bacterial systems. Positional
orthology supports rate analysis for AFP Afp2, AFP Afp3 and diffocin. The clearest adjusted
linear association is in Afp3. Afp2's evidence weakens on variable sites, and an association
was not detected in diffocin. The manuscript avoids treating two AFP proteins as independent
system-level replication or interpreting detected/non-detected associations as a tested
between-system difference.

The contribution connects conformational contact remodeling to protein evolution in a
structurally characterized bacterial machine. The manuscript documents orthology exclusions,
sequence-block uncertainty, unpropagated rate uncertainty and the departure between the
written extended-state covariate definition and the descriptors actually fitted. Exact
structural-copy provenance remains a stated limitation. Processed inputs, outputs and code
are prepared in a local archive; a persistent public link must be supplied before submission.

OpenAI Codex assisted with scientific editing, LaTeX preparation and presentation code used
to display archived results during revision. The author retains responsibility for the final
scientific content. No funding was received and no competing interests are declared.
